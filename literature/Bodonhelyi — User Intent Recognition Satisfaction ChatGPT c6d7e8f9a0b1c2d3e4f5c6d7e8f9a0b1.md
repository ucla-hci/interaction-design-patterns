# One Sentence
An eight-category, 24-subtype user intent taxonomy for LLM queries (Informational, Problem-Solving, Creative, Educational, Personal Interaction, Technical/Professional, Transactional, Ethical/Philosophical), with GPT-4 recognizing intent at 89.6% accuracy.

---

# More Sentences
Three-phase study (N=211 via Prolific): intent recognition accuracy, satisfaction with original vs. reformulated prompts, and comprehension of reformulation templates. Surprising result: users preferred their *original* prompts over LLM-reformulated ones (~54–57%), contradicting the prompt-improvement literature. Data skews heavily toward informational intents.

---

# Key Points

### Taxonomy granularity
Eight top-level intents with three subtypes each — finer than OpenAI's Asking/Doing/Expressing but coarser than Anthropic's 630 bottom-up categories. Notable inclusions for the format question: Decision Support and Planning (under Problem-Solving), Task Automation (under Transactional).

### Request-side, not response-side
Like all LLM intent taxonomies, this classifies what the user asked for — not what they do with the answer.

# Take-Away
- Confirms decision support and planning are first-class intent categories — supporting compare/decide as a task-battery purpose
- The intent-taxonomy tradition consistently stops at the request; the post-response task space is unclaimed
