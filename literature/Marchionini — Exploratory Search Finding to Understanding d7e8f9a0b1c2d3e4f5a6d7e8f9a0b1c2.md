# One Sentence
Divides search activity into lookup (well-defined queries retrieving discrete facts), learn (developing understanding through iteration), and investigate (open-ended analysis and synthesis) — the canonical taxonomy of information-seeking depth.

---

# More Sentences
Lookup ≈ known-item search: fact retrieval, verification, navigation. Learn and investigate constitute exploratory search: iterative, opportunistic, browsing-heavy, interwoven with learning from ill-structured problems. The three levels form a depth ordering — later work (Athukorala 2016) confirmed empirically that behavior differs measurably across them.

---

# Key Points

### Depth as the organizing axis
Lookup/learn/investigate is orthogonal to topic: any domain task decomposes into these depths. Maps onto the provisional six-purpose taxonomy: lookup ≈ locate; learn ≈ comprehend; investigate ≈ compare + synthesize.

### Format implications
Lookup rewards scannable, structured output (find the fact fast); learn rewards coherent prose (build a mental model); investigate rewards manipulable representations. The taxonomy *predicts* format × task interactions.

# Take-Away
- Independent theoretical support for separating locate from comprehend in the task battery — they are different search depths with different behavioral signatures
- The 2026 "New Shape of Search" paper found collapse rates statistically indistinguishable across lookup/learn/compare — an LLM-era caveat on the taxonomy's predictive power for episode structure (though not for format fit)
