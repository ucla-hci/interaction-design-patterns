**Survey** → **Approach type**: practitioner-facing study of GenUI tool adoption and workflow gaps

# One Sentence
Formative qualitative study with 37 UX professionals across four roles revealing how practitioners integrate GenUI tools into their workflows and identifying gaps between tool capabilities and user expectations.

---

# More Sentences
Participants completed a week-long individual mini-project exercise using a GenUI tool in role-specific tasks, keeping daily journals, followed by semi-structured interviews. The study surfaced role-specific patterns of use (designers, researchers, engineers, product managers use GenUI differently) and key gaps between GenUI capabilities and practitioner needs. Findings inform design recommendations for next-generation GenUI tools.

---

# Key Points

### Not a comparison study
Does not compare GenUI vs. text-response baselines. The study assumes GenUI is in use and asks how practitioners experience it.

### Gaps surfaced
The study identifies discrepancies between what GenUI tools produce and what practitioners need — implying current GenUI quality is not "good enough" for professional workflows without adaptation.

### Role differences
Different professional roles derive different value from GenUI tools — effectiveness is not uniform even within a single task domain.

# Other Notes
This is the closest thing to an ecological validity study of GenUI in professional practice. It doesn't measure "is GenUI better than text" but it does reveal that GenUI is valuable but imperfect in practitioner workflows.

# Take-Away
- Qualitative evidence that GenUI tools provide real (not marginal) value to practitioners, but with significant gaps
- The gaps identified (workflow integration, role-specific support, expectation mismatches) suggest current GenUI is useful but not plug-and-play
- Does not speak to the UI vs. text question directly
