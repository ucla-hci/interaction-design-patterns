# One Sentence
The first large-scale empirical account of ChatGPT usage: a three-way intent split (Asking 49% / Doing 40% / Expressing 11%) crossed with a 24-category topic taxonomy over seven domains.

---

# More Sentences
Analyzed ~1.1M randomly sampled consumer conversations (May 2024–June 2025) using automated LLM classifiers with no human access to content, validated against human-labeled WildChat samples. Asking = seeking information/advice to inform decisions; Doing = wanting a usable output artifact; Expressing = neither. Top topics: Practical Guidance 28.8%, Seeking Information 24.4%, Writing 23.3% (~78% combined). Coding is only 4.2% of messages. Non-work use rose from 47% to 73% over the year.

---

# Key Points

### Asking / Doing / Expressing
The intent taxonomy classifies the *request*, not the downstream use of the response. Asking (51.6% by June 2025) dominates and earns higher satisfaction (4.05 good:bad ratio vs. 2.76 for Doing).

### Ecological weights for a task battery
The category shares provide ecological frequency weights: information consumption (Asking) ≈ half of all usage; artifact production (Doing) ≈ 35–40%; writing is the single biggest work task.

### "Decision support" framing
The authors conclude ChatGPT functions primarily as an advisor/thinking partner rather than a task automator — i.e., the response's job is usually to *inform a human decision*.

# Other Notes
Convergence with Brehmer & Munzner's consume/produce top split (InfoVis): Asking ≈ consume, Doing ≈ produce. Independent literatures arriving at the same first cut.

# Take-Away
- Gives PatternGenUI ecological weights for which downstream purposes matter most (comprehend/locate/decide ≈ Asking share ≈ half of usage)
- The request-intent unit is complementary to, not a substitute for, a post-response task taxonomy
