**Quality grade**: A — TVCG; synthesis of two dozen prior taxonomies into one typology; heavily validated by subsequent use.
**Survey** → **Approach type**: multi-level task typology (why / how / what)

# One Sentence
Bridges low- and high-level task descriptions with a three-question typology — WHY is the task performed (consume vs. produce → search → query), HOW is it performed (methods), WHAT are the inputs/outputs — so that any task can be described completely at multiple altitudes.

---

# More Sentences
The WHY axis narrows from high-level (consume: present/discover/enjoy vs. produce) through mid-level (search: lookup/locate/browse/explore, crossed by whether target and location are known) to low-level (query: identify/compare/summarize). The critique motivating it: low-level taxonomies (Amar & Stasko) ignore why; high-level ones ignore how. Tasks are sequences — outputs of one become inputs of the next.

---

# Key Points

### Consume vs. produce as the first cut
The top-level WHY split is consume vs. produce — structurally identical to OpenAI's Asking vs. Doing (2025) derived from 1.1M ChatGPT conversations. Two unrelated methodologies, same first cut. Strong evidence this split belongs at the top of any downstream-task taxonomy.

### The search quadrant
Lookup (target known, location known) / locate (target known, location unknown) / browse (target unknown, location known) / explore (both unknown) — a finer decomposition of the "locate" purpose that predicts which layout topologies help.

### Multi-level description discipline
A task is not one label but a why/how/what triple, and tasks chain. Directly applicable to the format study: each probe should be specified at all three levels so effects can be attributed.

# Take-Away
- The strongest structural template for the final taxonomy: top split consume/produce, mid-level purposes, low-level probes
- Its search quadrant refines the locate purpose; its sequencing model supports chained tasks (per Pirolli & Card)
